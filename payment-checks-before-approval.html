3 Checks Before Every Supplier Payment

By Muhammed Hadhil · Published 22 Oct 2026 · 5 min read

One wrong payment can take weeks to recover. These three checks take a few minutes and prevent most payment errors, disputes and audit questions.

Paying suppliers quickly builds good relationships. Paying them correctly protects the business. Before any supplier payment leaves the account, I run three checks.

1. Does the invoice match the PO?

The purchase order is what the company agreed to buy. The invoice should match it.

  • Same supplier name and TRN
  • Same items or services
  • Same quantity, or less for partial deliveries
  • Same unit price and agreed terms

If the invoice is higher than the PO, stop and ask why. Price changes need approval before payment, not after.

2. Was it actually delivered?

An invoice is a request for payment, not proof of delivery. Look for evidence that the goods or services were received:

  • Signed delivery note or goods received note
  • Site or project confirmation for subcontracted work
  • Completion certificate for services

This is the classic three-way match: PO, delivery and invoice must all agree.

3. Is the VAT correct?

Input VAT can only be recovered on a valid tax invoice. Check the key details:

CheckWhat to look for
TitleThe words "Tax Invoice" are clearly shown
Supplier TRN15-digit TRN, and it matches the supplier's registration
Your detailsYour company's legal name, address and TRN
Numbering and datesUnique invoice number, invoice date and supply date
VAT calculationCorrect rate (5% standard) and VAT amount shown in AED

Under the VAT rules, a tax invoice should generally be issued within 14 days of the supply. Late or incomplete invoices are worth flagging to the supplier straight away.

Why it matters

These checks take a few extra minutes. They save hours of follow-ups, credit notes and audit questions later. Strong controls aren't about slowing payments down. They're about paying the right amount, to the right supplier, once.
