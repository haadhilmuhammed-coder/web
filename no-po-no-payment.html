No PO, No Payment: Why Every Purchase Needs a Purchase Order

By Muhammed Hadhil · Published 22 Oct 2026 · 4 min read

"Can we just pay this invoice? The PO will come later." Every finance person has heard it. Here is why one simple rule, no PO, no payment, matters.

When purchases happen without a purchase order, finance finds out about the cost only when the invoice arrives. By then, the money is already committed, the price is already agreed and the budget may already be gone.

What a good purchase order does

  • Confirms price and quantity before money is committed
  • Shows who approved the spend and that they had the authority to do so
  • Gives finance something to match the invoice and delivery against
  • Makes cost tracking possible by project, department or cost centre
  • Protects in disputes, because the agreed terms are in writing

What goes wrong without one

Without a POResult
No agreed priceSupplier invoices at a higher rate, and there is nothing to dispute it with
No approvalSpend goes over budget before anyone notices
No project codeCosts land in the wrong project and profit reports are wrong
No record of the orderDuplicate orders and duplicate payments

How to introduce the rule

  1. Agree it with management first. The rule only works if leaders follow it too
  2. Set approval limits. Who can approve how much
  3. Allow clear exceptions. Utilities, government fees and true emergencies, with approval after the fact
  4. Tell suppliers. Invoices without a valid PO number will be returned
  5. Make raising a PO easy. A simple form or system so the rule doesn't slow the business down

It may feel slow at first. But a PO policy saves money, disputes and audit questions, and gives management a real view of committed costs before the invoices arrive.
