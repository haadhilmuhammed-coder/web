How End-of-Service Gratuity Is Calculated in the UAE

By Muhammed Hadhil · Published 22 Oct 2026 · Last checked 22 Oct 2026 · 6 min read

Gratuity is one of the most misunderstood payments in UAE payroll. Here is how it works for private-sector employees, with a worked example.

End-of-service gratuity is paid to private-sector employees when their employment ends, under the UAE Labour Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021). The rules are simple once you know the basics.

The basics

  • Basic salary only. Housing, transport and other allowances are not included
  • Daily wage = last basic monthly salary ÷ 30
  • First 5 years: 21 days' basic wage for each year of service
  • After 5 years: 30 days' basic wage for each additional year
  • Minimum service: at least 1 year of continuous service; part years after that are paid pro-rata
  • Cap: total gratuity cannot exceed 2 years' wages
  • Unpaid leave: days of unpaid leave are not counted as service
  • Payment: end-of-service dues should be paid within 14 days of the employment ending

Worked example

An employee with a basic salary of AED 12,000 leaves after 8 full years of service.

Daily wage = 12,000 ÷ 30 = AED 400 First 5 years = 5 × 21 × 400 = AED 42,000 Next 3 years = 3 × 30 × 400 = AED 36,000 Total gratuity = AED 78,000 Cap check: 2 years' wages = 24 × 12,000 = AED 288,000 78,000 is below the cap, so the full amount is payable.

Resignation and termination

Under the current law, gratuity is calculated the same way whether the employee resigns or is terminated, provided they have completed at least one year of service. The older reductions for resignation no longer apply. Some cases, such as dismissal for specific serious reasons set out in the law, need separate review.

What finance teams should do

Provision every month

Gratuity is a liability that builds up every month. Record a monthly provision so the cost is spread correctly, instead of discovering it on an employee's last day.

Keep the staff master accurate

The calculation is only as good as the data: joining date, current basic salary and unpaid leave days. Review the staff master regularly, and include active staff only in provision schedules.

Reconcile the provision yearly

At year-end, recalculate gratuity for every active employee and compare it to the balance in the books. Adjust the difference and keep the schedule for audit.

Tip: build the gratuity schedule in Excel with formulas for years of service, the 21/30-day split and the cap. Update the salary and dates each month, and the provision updates itself.
