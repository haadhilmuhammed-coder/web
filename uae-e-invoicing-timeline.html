UAE E-Invoicing: Key Dates and How Finance Teams Can Prepare

By Muhammed Hadhil · Published 22 Oct 2026 · Last checked 22 Oct 2026 · 6 min read

The UAE is moving to mandatory electronic invoicing in phases from January 2027. The first deadline, for appointing an Accredited Service Provider, is 30 October 2026.

Under the national e-invoicing system, businesses will exchange invoices electronically in a structured data format (XML), so that invoice data can be processed automatically between supplier, buyer and the tax authority. A PDF sent by email will no longer count as an e-invoice.

Who goes live, and when

PhaseWho is coveredAppoint an ASP byMandatory go-live
Phase 1Businesses with annual revenue of AED 50 million or more30 Oct 20261 Jan 2027
Phase 2Businesses with annual revenue below AED 50 million31 Mar 20271 Jul 2027
Phase 3Government entities31 Mar 20271 Oct 2027

ASP means Accredited Service Provider: an approved provider that sends and receives e-invoices on your behalf. According to the published guidance, the requirement applies whether or not a business is VAT-registered, unless it is specifically excluded.

Why this matters for finance teams

E-invoicing is not just a tax change. It changes how every invoice moves through accounts payable and accounts receivable.

  • Accounts receivable: sales invoices must be generated in the required structured format and sent through your ASP
  • Accounts payable: supplier invoices will arrive as data, which can be matched to POs and receipts faster
  • Data quality: errors in customer or supplier details will surface immediately instead of at audit

What you can start now

1. Clean your master data

Check every active supplier and customer record: legal name exactly as on the trade licence, full address and 15-digit TRN where they are VAT-registered. Close duplicate and inactive records.

2. Check your ERP or accounting system

Ask your software provider whether your system can produce structured e-invoices, and whether it already connects to an ASP. If not, ask for their timeline.

3. Shortlist ASPs early

Compare providers on cost, ERP integration, support and data security. Leaving it until the deadline limits your choice.

4. Review your invoice templates

Make sure current tax invoices already meet the existing requirements, such as the words "Tax Invoice", supplier TRN, sequential numbering and VAT shown in AED. Clean invoices today make the move easier.

5. Plan training

Sales, procurement and finance staff all touch invoices. A short session on the new process will prevent rejected invoices later.

Quick check: know which phase your company falls into, and who in your company owns the e-invoicing project.
